Real Numbers

Counting Numbers

Say we want to know how many apples are in a bag. We count them. We pair each of them with numbers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, etc until the last one. The number paired to the last one shows how many apples are in the bag.

The numbers that we use to count are called counting numbers. They forms an increasing infinite sequence.

1,2,3,4,5,6…

The sequence of counting numbers is an increasing sequence. A number is greater than all of other numbers that come before it, because it stands for more objects. Hence 2 is greater than 1 or 2 > 1, or 6 is greater than 3, 6 > 3.

On the other hand, a number that comes before is less than that which comes after. That’s because it stands for less things. Hence 1 is less than 2 or 1 < 2, and 3 is less than 6 or 3 < 6. And we have the least counting number: 1.

The sequence of counting number is infinite means that for any number in it there will always be another number after it. After 10 we have 11, after a million we have a million and one. Thus no matter how many object we have we’d get a number for it.
